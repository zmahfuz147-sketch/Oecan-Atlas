The World of Crabs

From tiny shore-dwellers to giant spider crabs and strange animals living beside deep-sea vents, crabs have conquered an astonishing range of habitats. Meet the true crabs, their crab-like relatives, and the species that reveal how inventive life beneath the waves can be.

01 · The numbers

How many crabs exist?

The answer depends on what scientists mean by “crab.” Taxonomists distinguish true crabs from several crab-shaped relatives.

🦀

Living true crabs · Brachyura

Approx. 7,600+

Described living species of true crabs

A 2024 review of crab fossils and evolution reports more than 7,600 known living Brachyura species. This is a rounded scientific snapshot, not a permanent final count. New species are described, and names are revised when researchers compare specimens and genetic evidence. Hermit crabs, king crabs, and horseshoe crabs are not included in the true-crab total. 02 · Meet the groups

Five animals people call “crabs”

Some are true crabs. Others evolved a similar body shape along a different branch of the arthropod family tree.

🦀
BrachyuraTrue crab

True crabs

Infraorder Brachyura

Size: Approx. a few millimetres to several dozen centimetres across the carapace, depending on species.

Habitat: Marine shores, reefs, estuaries, freshwater, and land.

Fact: Their abdomen is short and folded beneath the body; the front pair of legs usually forms claws.

Explore the classification →
🐚
AnomuraCrab-like relative

Hermit crabs

Superfamily Paguroidea

Size: Usually small to medium, from tiny marine species to much larger forms; size varies widely.

Habitat: Rocky shores, reefs, sandy bottoms, deep sea, and—in some lineages—land.

Fact: Many protect their soft, curved abdomen inside an empty snail shell, changing shells as they grow.

Explore Anomura →
👑
AnomuraCrab-like relative

King crabs

Family Lithodidae

Size: Red king crabs can reach approx. 1.5 m in leg span and approx. 28 cm in carapace length.

Habitat: Cold North Pacific waters, from shallow coastal grounds to deeper seafloor.

Fact: Despite their crab-like shape, king crabs belong to Anomura and are relatives of hermit crabs—not true crabs.

🕷️
BrachyuraLong-legged

Spider crabs

Common name used for several brachyuran families

Size: The Japanese spider crab can reach approx. 3.8 m in leg span and approx. 30 cm across the carapace.

Habitat: Coastal seabeds and continental slopes; the giant Japanese species lives in the northwestern Pacific.

Fact: Young Japanese spider crabs may decorate their shells with kelp or other objects for camouflage.

🌙
XiphosuraNot a true crab

Horseshoe crabs

Order Xiphosura

Size: Atlantic horseshoe crabs can reach approx. 60 cm in total length, including the tail.

Habitat: Shallow coastal waters with sandy or muddy bottoms; adults often visit beaches to spawn.

Fact: They are not crustacean crabs. Their closest living relatives are arachnids such as spiders and scorpions.

“Crab” is a common name, not a single taxonomic group. The word can describe true crabs and several unrelated or more distant crab-shaped relatives.

03 · Asia · Deep water

Crabs of Asian deep seas

Japan’s Pacific margin, the Sea of Japan, and the Okinawa Trough hold remarkable animals—from enormous scavengers to small vent-dwellers with bacterial partners.

ASIA · 01

Japanese spider crab

Macrocheira kaempferi

  • Depth: approx. 200–550 m
  • Size: carapace up to approx. 30 cm; leg span up to approx. 3.8 m
  • Range: northwestern Pacific, around Japan

One of the largest living crabs by leg span. It walks across deep seabeds searching for dead animals, decaying material, and algae. Young crabs may attach kelp or other objects to their shells as camouflage.

Sources [4] ↗
ASIA · 02

Hairy vent crab

Shinkaia crosnieri

  • Depth records: approx. 692–1,931 m
  • Size: carapace length approx. 5 cm
  • Habitat: hydrothermal vents, especially the Okinawa Trough

This pale, bristly galatheid is a squat-lobster relative in Anomura, not a true Brachyura crab. Chemosynthetic bacteria live on its hair-like setae, and research shows the animal digests and absorbs these bacterial partners as a food source.

Sources [7] ↗
ASIA · 03

Red snow crab

Chionoecetes japonicus

  • Depth: approx. 480–2,300 m in Japanese records
  • Size: up to approx. 10 cm carapace width in SeaLifeBase records
  • Range: northwestern Pacific, including Japanese waters

Its deep red colour and swollen branchial regions help distinguish it from the more familiar snow crab, Chionoecetes opilio. It is associated with cold, deep seafloor habitats.

Sources [8] ↗

Depth records and maximum sizes can differ between databases, locations, sexes, and survey methods. Values above are approximate reference ranges, not limits for every individual.

04 · Europe · North Atlantic

Crabs of European deep waters

The continental slopes of the Northeast Atlantic shelter red deep-water crabs, burrowing species, and carriers that transport living camouflage on their backs.

EUROPE · 01

Deep-sea red crab

Chaceon affinis

  • Depth: approx. 400–1,500 m in the Northeast Atlantic
  • Size: female carapace length up to approx. 17.7 cm; males up to approx. 15.5 cm in SeaLifeBase records
  • Habitat: continental slopes, seamounts, and deep-water banks

A large geryonid true crab found on deep Atlantic bottoms. It is commercially fished in parts of the Northeast Atlantic, so its fisheries and population data are monitored.

Sources [9] ↗
EUROPE · 02

Three-spined deep-sea crab

Geryon trispinosus

  • Depth: usually approx. 30–700 m; records farther south reach approx. 1,800 m
  • Size: male carapace width approx. 9.8 cm
  • Habitat: soft sediment on Northeast Atlantic bottoms

This red-to-brick-red crab lives on soft seabeds and burrows into the sediment. The HELCOM information sheet notes that females are slightly smaller than males and that available population data are limited.

Source [10] ↗
EUROPE · 03

Carrier crab

Paromola cuvieri

  • Depth: usually approx. 150–345 m; recorded down to approx. 1,000 m
  • Size: carapace length up to approx. 16 cm
  • Range: eastern Atlantic, from European waters southward

Its specially positioned rear legs can hold objects over its back. Sponges, corals, and sea fans have been observed being carried, probably helping the crab camouflage itself and deter predators.

Source [11] ↗

The Northeast Atlantic depth and size ranges above are based on regional surveys and species accounts; local records may fall outside the typical range.

05 · Deep time

Famous crabs from the fossil record

Fossils preserve experiments in crab shape that no longer exist. These extinct species are known from fossil material, not living populations.

FOSSIL · 01

Callichimaera perplexa

“The beautiful chimera”

  • Time: approx. 95–90 million years ago, Cretaceous
  • Size: carapace length approx. 6.6–15.1 mm; width approx. 3.8–9.6 mm in the studied sample
  • Found in: Colombia and the United States

Fossils only. This small, unusual true crab had large exposed eyes and paddle-like swimming legs. Its body looked unlike the familiar crab shape, and researchers interpret it as a specialised swimmer rather than a larval stage.

Source [12] ↗
FOSSIL · 02

Cretapsara athanata

A small Cretaceous crab preserved in amber

  • Time: approx. 99 million years ago, Cretaceous
  • Size: Needs verification
  • Found in: Myanmar, preserved in amber

Fossils only. The amber specimen preserves delicate details, including eyes, antennae, mouthparts, and gills. The published description establishes its age and locality; the exact body measurement is marked for verification here rather than estimated from an illustration.

Source [13] ↗

A 2024 review records more than approx. 3,000 fossil true-crab species across Jurassic, Cretaceous, and Cenozoic deposits. The fossil record is incomplete and continues to change as new material is described. Source [1] ↗

06 · Life cycle

How crabs reproduce

Many crabs begin life as tiny planktonic larvae. Their route from egg to adult includes dramatic changes in body shape and repeated moulting.

01 · COURTSHIP

Finding a mate

In many species, courtship and mating are linked to the female’s moult. A male may guard or carry a female before she sheds her old shell, then mate with her while her new shell is still soft. This pattern varies across crab groups.

02 · EGGS

A protected brood

After fertilisation, the female attaches the eggs to swimmerets beneath her abdomen. The egg mass can look like a sponge, so an egg-carrying female is often called a “sponge crab.” She carries and ventilates the brood until hatching.

03 · ZOEA

Tiny drifting larvae

In many marine true crabs, eggs hatch into zoea larvae. These small, often spiny larvae drift in the plankton and feed as they develop, moulting between larval stages.

04 · MEGALOPA

The crab-like transition

The zoea changes into a megalopa: a larval stage with a more crab-like body and developing legs. It can swim and crawl before settling into a suitable bottom habitat.

05 · MOULTING

Growing a new shell

Young crabs settle and moult into miniature juveniles. Because the hard external skeleton cannot expand like skin, a crab must shed it and harden a larger new shell as it grows.

A NOTE ON VARIATION

Not every crab follows the same script

Larval stages, egg-carrying behaviour, timing, and habitat shifts differ among species. Some freshwater and land crabs have shortened development or hatch as more developed young.

The zoea → megalopa → juvenile sequence is a common marine-crab pattern, illustrated here with blue crab and red king crab life-history references. Sources [14] ↗

07 · Food webs

What do crabs eat?

There is no single crab diet. Many species are flexible feeders, switching between food sources as conditions change.

SCAVENGERS

The ocean clean-up crew

Many spider crabs and other seabed walkers eat dead or decaying fish, crustaceans, invertebrates, algae, and organic scraps that settle on the bottom. Scavenging helps recycle nutrients.

PREDATORS

Claws for catching prey

Predatory crabs may crush molluscs, capture worms, attack other crustaceans, or take small fish. Their claws can grip, tear, cut, or crush food depending on their shape.

GRAZERS & OMNIVORES

A varied menu

Some species browse on algae and plant material; others mix these with carrion and small animals. Opportunistic feeding lets many crabs use whatever suitable food is available.

DEEP-SEA SPECIALISTS

Food from bacteria

At hydrothermal vents, Shinkaia crosnieri obtains nutrition from chemosynthetic bacteria living on its bristly setae. This is a striking example of an animal using microbial partners as a food source.

08 · Adaptations

Behaviour and special abilities

Crabs use camouflage, movement, shelter, and partnerships to solve the everyday problems of survival.

CAMOUFLAGE

Wear the seafloor

Young Japanese spider crabs may attach kelp or other objects to their shells. Carrier crabs use their rear legs to hold sponges, corals, or sea fans above the body.

Sources [4] ↗
MIGRATION

Moving with the seasons

Red king crabs make seasonal movements between shallower mating and moulting grounds and deeper feeding areas. The route and timing depend on the population and local conditions.

Source [6] ↗
BURROWING

Hide beneath the sediment

Geryon trispinosus lives on soft seabeds and burrows into sediment. Burying can provide shelter and help an animal remain less visible on an exposed bottom.

Source [10] ↗
DEFENSIVE PARTNERSHIPS

A living shield

Some hermit crabs carry sea anemones on their shells. The anemone’s stinging cells can help deter predators, while the anemone gains transport and access to food scraps. Researchers describe some of these behaviours as animal-assisted tool use; it is not the same as using a manufactured tool.

Source [15] ↗

09 · Did you know?

Five facts to take to the surface

The crab family tree is stranger—and older—than its familiar sideways walk suggests.

FACT · 01

Crab shape evolved more than once

The crab-like body plan evolved independently in several crustacean lineages. This repeated evolutionary change is called carcinisation.

FACT · 02

Horseshoe crabs are not crabs

They are chelicerates, more closely related to spiders and scorpions than to crustacean crabs. Their blue, copper-based blood is important in biomedical testing.

FACT · 03

A crab with a huge reach

The Japanese spider crab can have an approximate leg span of 3.8 m, although its long legs are relatively delicate.

FACT · 04

A meal growing on its body

The deep-sea Shinkaia crosnieri digests bacterial partners living on its bristles, gaining nutrition from microbes associated with hydrothermal vents.

FACT · 05

The fossil record is full of surprises

More than approx. 3,000 fossil true-crab species have been reported in the scientific literature, including forms unlike any living crab.
